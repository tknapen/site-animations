# Aqil, Knapen and Dumoulin (2021)

Divisive normalization unifies disparate response signatures throughout the human visual hierarchy.
PNAS 118(46), e2108713118. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2108713118 (open access, PMC8609633)

What the clip takes from it:

- The DN pRF model (Materials and Methods, "pRF Models"; Fig. 1D): activation and normalization pRFs are
  isotropic Gaussians G1 and G2 centred on the same position, with sizes sigma_1 and sigma_2 and amplitudes
  a and c, an activation constant b and a normalization constant d. The predicted response is the ratio of
  activation and normalization minus b/d, so that it is zero without a stimulus.
- Results, "DN Model Constants Modulate Suppression and Compression" (Fig. 3 A and B): a nonzero b is
  necessary for a suppressive centre-surround pRF profile, and raising b strengthens suppression; d
  inversely modulates the compression of the response function (high d: nearly linear; low d: compressive).
- Materials and Methods, "PRF Profiles and Response Function": the pRF profile is a one-dimensional slice
  of the pRF shape in visual space; the response function is the model response to the fraction of the
  activation and normalization pRFs being stimulated. Both are simplifications of the full model.

The typeset equations were not in the text the agent could read. The model equation was checked against
prfpy (prfpy/model.py, Norm_Iso2DGaussianModel.return_prediction).
