# Brief: divisive normalization (website clip)

For the Knapen Lab website (tknapen.github.io): the page on computational models and the page of
Aqil, Knapen and Dumoulin (2021), PNAS 118, e2108713118, doi 10.1073/pnas.2108713118.

What the viewer should understand: one computation, a ratio of an activation pRF and a larger
normalization pRF, produces two response signatures that used to need separate models. The activation
constant b brings out surround suppression in the pRF profile; the normalization constant d (inversely)
sets how compressive the response function is.

Audience: visitors with a neuroscience background, not necessarily pRF modellers. No narration; the clip
is silent and sits in a page with a caption, about 900 px wide. About 20 seconds. Light and dark renders.

Sources: the paper's model equation (Materials and Methods, "pRF Models"), and its simulations of the
pRF profile and the response function (Results, "DN Model Constants Modulate Suppression and Compression",
Fig. 3 A and B; Materials and Methods, "PRF Profiles and Response Function").
